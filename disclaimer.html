2. Hardware Modification Risk — Read This First

Calibration writes data to your controller's own internal memory. Any modification of controller firmware or stored calibration carries an inherent, irreducible risk — including, in rare cases, the risk that a controller stops working correctly or permanently. You use the calibration feature entirely at your own risk.

KontrolKalib's calibration feature is built on a community reverse-engineering of the vendor calibration commands used by certain Sony controllers, and is provided on a best-effort basis. While we implement multiple safety measures, we cannot and do not guarantee that calibration will succeed, that it will improve any given controller, or that it will leave every controller in a working state.

Safety measures built into the App include, without limitation:

  • Genuine-hardware check — only controllers that pass an authenticity check are calibrated. Counterfeit or "clone" controllers are refused, because they do not implement these commands and writing to them is unsafe.
  • Firmware check — controllers running firmware known to behave unpredictably are blocked from calibration.
  • Charge check — a battery-level confirmation guards permanent saves, because a write interrupted by a flat battery can damage a controller.
  • Fail-safe writes — the App verifies the controller's response at every step and stops immediately, sending no further data, if it receives any unexpected response.
  • Temporary-first — calibration is applied temporarily by default and reverts when the controller is fully powered off, so you can test the result before choosing to save it permanently.

Despite these measures, you acknowledge that firmware-level writes can never be made completely risk-free. Do not calibrate a controller you cannot afford to lose. We strongly recommend using the feature only on a genuine, out-of-warranty controller and keeping the controller connected and charged throughout the process.

3. Calibration Compensates — It Does Not Repair

Calibration re-centres the electronics; it cannot repair a worn stick.

Analog-stick drift is most often caused by physical wear of the potentiometers inside a controller. Software calibration can compensate for minor wear by adjusting the stored centre and range, but it does not and cannot repair worn or damaged hardware. Severe, jumping, or worsening drift indicates a hardware fault that requires physical repair or module replacement. Any improvement produced by calibration may be partial or temporary. KontrolKalib makes no promise that any particular controller's drift will be fixed, reduced, or kept fixed.

4. Diagnostic Results Are Indicative Only

The drift scores, verdicts (OK, Minor, Significant, Severe), range and circularity figures, and before/after comparisons produced by the App are indicative measurements derived from the data your device reports. They depend on how you perform each test and on the accuracy of the controller's own sensors. They are provided to help you make your own decisions and must not be relied upon as a definitive assessment of any hardware.
